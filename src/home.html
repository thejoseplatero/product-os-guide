The case

Uber Eats has strong overall growth, but weekday lunchtime orders from 11:30am to 1:30pm are 30% lower than dinner orders.

That observation is everything you have. There is no backlog, no personas, no research, no strategy doc. That is the point. Every artifact gets built by you, in order, from discovery through go to market. Call it the lunch gap.

Work the phases in sequence. Each guide uses what earlier guides produced: the interviews feed the personas, the personas feed the value proposition, the value proposition feeds the cut, the cut feeds the PRD. Skip around later. The first pass is a chain.

How each guide works

Read the TLDR so you know where you are going. Read the full idea slowly. Then work the steps. Every step has the same four rows: a prompt you type into Claude (Search), what you should come away with (Get), the work you do on the lunch gap (Build), and the rule or artifact you write into your doc in your own words (Capture).

The sentence you write is the whole point. The search gives you the material. The learning is the compression. At the end of each skill you paste your notes into Claude and ask for a skill file. What comes out is your playbook with a header on top. You own it because you wrote it.

Keep one doc

A Google Doc, a Notion page, or a text file. One heading per skill. Your rules and artifacts go under the heading.

Let Claude play the users

You do not have office workers to interview. Ask Claude to play them, honestly: "play a desk-bound office worker in a city center, answer only from past behavior." Real research beats simulated research, but simulated research beats none, and the method transfers.

Compare with the real one at the end

After you build a skill, open the Product OS version. Ask what it has that yours does not, and what yours has that it does not. That comparison is your review.
