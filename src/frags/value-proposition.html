Value Proposition Canvas and Statements

TLDR: Map one customer's jobs, pains, and gains against what you offer, check the fit, then compress the match into one sharp sentence. Reach for this once you have a job statement and personas, and before you touch business models or features.

The full idea

You now know who struggles with weekday lunch and what job they are hiring for. This guide answers the next question: what exactly would you offer them, and why would it beat what they do today? The tool is Alexander Osterwalder's Value Proposition Canvas, from the Strategyzer books. Think of it as a zoom lens on one customer segment: not the whole business, just the exchange of value between one type of person and one offer.

The canvas has two sides. The right side is the customer profile: their jobs (what they are trying to get done), their pains (what hurts or blocks them today), and their gains (what a great outcome looks like). The left side is your value map: products and services, pain relievers, and gain creators. The whole game is fit: does each thing on your side connect to something real on their side? Anything on the left with no partner on the right is a feature nobody asked for. Any severe pain on the right with nothing pointing at it is a hole in your offer.

The order matters, and it is the same rule every canvas shares: customer side first, always. Fill the left side first and you get a brochure, a list of things you would enjoy building, decorated with imagined customers. You already did the honest work: the job statement from JTBD Framing and the pains and gains sitting in your interview evidence. This canvas is where they land. And run one canvas per segment: Desk-bound Dana and On-shift Sam do not share pains, so they do not share a canvas.

For the lunch gap, Dana's canvas might read: job, get a real lunch without breaking the flow of the workday. Pains, unpredictable arrival times, fees that feel heavy on a $12 meal, decision fatigue at 11:45. Gains, food landing inside a known 15 minute window, one tap reorder. A pain reliever has to answer one of those pains by name: a guaranteed delivery window answers unpredictability; nothing on that list is answered by "AI powered recommendations".

Once the fit is real, compress it into a value proposition statement: For [target customer] who [statement of need], [product] is [product or feature description] that [statement of benefit]. A common fifth clause, unlike [alternative], makes the comparison explicit. The "unlike" clause is the honest part, because the alternative is the bar. For Dana it is not another app; it is the sad desk sandwich and the office microwave. Beat that, or nothing else on the canvas matters.

The most common mistake is writing the value side in your own vocabulary instead of the customer's, "seamless logistics optimization" for a person who just wants lunch to show up at 12:15. Every box is also still a guess with a confidence level, not a fact. As a PM, this one page is your argument for why anyone will care, and every feature you fund later should trace back to a pain or gain written on it.

Build it step by step

STEP 1

What is the Value Proposition Canvas?

15 min
Search
Explain Osterwalder's Value Proposition Canvas in simple English: the customer side with jobs, pains, and gains, the value side with products, pain relievers, and gain creators, and what "fit" means between them. Why must the customer side be filled first, and what goes wrong when a team starts on the value side? Use a delivery marketplace at lunchtime as the example.
Get
The six boxes and what belongs in each, the meaning of fit, and the rule that customer side comes first because a canvas filled product-first is a brochure.
Build
Set up two blank canvases in your doc, one for each persona from Personas and Archetypes. Write each persona's name and the job statement from JTBD Framing at the top of its canvas. Fill nothing else yet.
Capture
Write your ordering rule in one sentence. A stem: "I fill the customer side first because ..."
STEP 2

Fill the customer side from evidence

25 min
Search
Here is my persona, my job statement, and my interview evidence about weekday lunch: [paste for one persona]. Fill the customer side of a Value Proposition Canvas: functional, social, and emotional jobs, then pains ranked by severity, then gains ranked by how much they matter. Only use what the evidence supports, and flag anything you had to guess.
Get
A customer profile where every job, pain, and gain traces to your interviews, with pains ranked by severity and guesses flagged instead of hidden.
Build
Do this for both personas. Cut or mark as low confidence anything flagged as a guess. Then circle the top two pains on each canvas: these are the pains your offer must answer or it has no reason to exist.
Capture
Write the two circled pains per persona into your doc with the quote or observation that backs each one.
STEP 3

Design the value side and check fit

25 min
Search
Here is the customer side of my canvas for [persona]: [paste]. Propose a value side for a lunch offer on Uber Eats: products or services, pain relievers, gain creators. Then check fit line by line: which pain or gain does each item connect to, which items connect to nothing, and which severe pains are left unanswered?
Get
A value side where every item points at a named pain or gain, a list of orphan items to cut, and a list of unanswered severe pains to solve or admit.
Build
Delete every orphan item, however clever it sounds. For each unanswered severe pain, either add a reliever or write "unsolved" next to it honestly. Repeat for the second persona and note where the two value sides differ: that difference is why they needed separate canvases.
Capture
Write your fit rule in one sentence. A stem: "A feature stays on the canvas only when ..."
STEP 4

Compress it into a statement

15 min
Search
Turn this Value Proposition Canvas for [persona] into a value proposition statement in the format: For [target customer] who [statement of need], [product] is [product or feature description] that [statement of benefit]. A common fifth clause, unlike [alternative], makes the comparison explicit. Write three candidate versions. The alternative must be what this person actually does for lunch today, not a competing app. Then tell me which version is sharpest and why.
Get
Three candidate statements per persona with an honest "unlike" clause, and a view on which is sharpest.
Build
Pick one statement per persona and stress test it: read it aloud and ask whether Dana or Sam would recognize themselves in the first clause, and whether the named alternative is really what your interviews said they do today. Fix any clause that fails.
Capture
Write the final statement for each persona into your doc. These statements and both canvases feed straight into the Product-Market Fit Canvas next.
STEP 5

Pair the proposition with a solution hypothesis

25 min
Search
Explain the difference between a value proposition statement and a solution hypothesis. Value proposition: "For [target customer] who [statement of need or opportunity], [product name] is [product or feature description] that [statement of benefit]." Solution hypothesis: "Changing [what your analysis indicates is the problem] into [what change you think will solve that problem] will [the effect it will have on your KPI]." Why do these two belong on the same page?
Get
The proposition is a promise, written in the customer's language, and it has no number in it. The solution hypothesis is a bet, written in your language, and it must have a number in it. They belong together because the proposition is unfalsifiable on its own. Pairing them is what stops a value proposition from being a slogan: the hypothesis underneath it names the change you are making and the metric that would prove or kill it.
Build
Write the lunch gap value proposition in the four-part form, filling every bracket from work you already did: target customer from Personas and Archetypes, the need from the JTBD job statement, the description from your canvas fit, and the benefit from the gain creators. Directly underneath it, write the solution hypothesis in the three-part form. The problem clause comes from your research hypothesis, the change clause is the specific thing you would build, and the effect clause names the metric and the direction. Then check the pair: if the benefit in the proposition and the effect in the hypothesis are not the same thing seen from two sides, one of them is wrong.
Capture
Write the paired proposition and solution hypothesis into your doc. Then add one sentence that starts "If the KPI in my hypothesis moved but the benefit in my proposition did not show up, that would mean ..."

Make it a skill

Your doc now holds two evidence-based canvases with fit checked line by line, the circled pains with their backing quotes, and one value proposition statement per persona.

Turn this into a Claude skill file called SKILL.md named value-proposition. Keep my words and my examples. Add the frontmatter with a name and a description that says when to use it. Do not add rules I did not write.

Then test it: paste your On-shift Sam persona and job statement into a fresh chat and prompt "use my value-proposition skill to build the canvas and statement for this segment".

Smells to watch for

  • Value side written before the customer side. That is a brochure, not a canvas.
  • The value proposition in the product's vocabulary instead of the segment's.
  • Pain relievers with no matching pain: features looking for a reason.
  • "Unlike" pointing at a competitor app when the real alternative is a packed lunch or skipping the meal.
  • One canvas stretched over two segments, or every box confidently full with zero flagged guesses.
